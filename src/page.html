About these numbers

  • Each count is a floor. The cameras come from DeFlock and other OpenStreetMap volunteers. A camera counts only once someone has found it and mapped it, so a neighborhood nobody has walked looks less watched than it is. Know of one that's missing? Add it on DeFlock, and it shows up here the next day.
  • What they do. Automated license plate readers photograph every passing car and log its plate, where it was and when, whether or not anyone in it is suspected of anything.
  • Comparing districts. Districts on the same electoral map (council, supervisor, Congress, state legislature, trustee areas) each hold about the same number of residents, so their counts compare fairly. Cities and school districts vary widely in size.
  • Council districts and trustee areas count only cameras inside the city or school district that drew them. Elsewhere in the county collects cameras outside every area on a map; on the city map, that's unincorporated land.
  • The districts, and the officeholders named with them, come from District Lookup, a free tool that finds every district an address is in.
  • Updated every day from OpenStreetMap. The CSV has every number on this page; the JSON adds the location, maker and operator of each camera.
